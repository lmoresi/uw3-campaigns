Another case, from a 3-D micromorphic notch model under the units system: a stress-to-yield ratio built from the model's own expressions and evaluated at one point.

| expression | `uw.function.evaluate` returned |
|---|---|
| `stokes.constitutive_model.viscosity` (viscoplastic η_eff) | 8.20e22 (Pa s: SI) |
| the Drucker–Prager yield stress (with a micromorphic shift) | 4.59e8 (Pa: SI) |
| `stokes.Unknowns.Einv2` | 3.83e-8 (neither the non-dimensional ~0.5 nor 1/s; per year?) |

So `2 * viscosity * Einv2 / yield_stress`, evaluated as one expression, came out around 1e14 everywhere: the pieces are in different unit systems. Dividing two viscosities (`viscosity / shear_viscosity_0`) does give a sensible ratio, since both come back in the same system.

This makes any physically meaningful derived quantity, such as a yield indicator or stress over strength, a trap unless every factor carries the same flavour. It fits #771 (SI whenever an expression carries a dimensional constant) and #843 (units borrowed from another variable).
