### #98 (not probed): what we already know, and what one run would settle
- It is not about rotated free slip. The cases that go wrong impose only the normal velocity through a penalty natural condition (1e8) where the benchmark prescribes the whole velocity; the author's own conclusion is that the boundary-condition class, not the normal, is the main effect. The full-velocity cases (`essential`, `natural_full`) agree with each other to 0.02.
- Even the `essential` baseline has an inner-boundary pressure error of 2.0 (m = -1) and 1.8 (m = 3) at cell size 1/8, and the author's radial-grading runs trade inner error for outer error. That reads as resolution of a pressure that varies steeply near the inner wall with a continuous P1 pressure, not as a defect.
- From this week's measurements on the rotated solve: constant pressure is an exact null vector under the default (facet-weighted) normal and only approximately one under the analytic normal on a faceted wall, so a pressure offset between normal choices is expected on a coarse shell; and a normal-only wall leaves rigid rotations in play on a shell, which a full-velocity benchmark does not have. Neither was controlled in the issue's comparison.
- The run that would settle it: the Thieulot shell with the full velocity prescribed (essential), at three resolutions (for example 1/8, 1/12, 1/16), reporting the inner-boundary pressure L2 error and its observed order with the gauge fixed the same way each time. If it converges at the expected rate, close as not a defect; if it does not, it is a pressure-recovery question at a curved Dirichlet wall. This needs a Thieulot reference in `uw.analytic` (the library has none; the spherical Kramer references merged this week are the nearest thing and would serve the same purpose with free slip or zero slip).
